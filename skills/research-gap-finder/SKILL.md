# Research Gap Finder

## Purpose

Identify evidence-backed opportunities in a defined literature set and assess whether each could matter. A gap is not automatically novel, important, or a contribution.

## When to Use

- A literature matrix exists and the next step is to compare coverage or limitations.
- A proposed research direction needs a novelty and significance challenge.
- Researchers need to distinguish a search blind spot from a defensible gap.

## Inputs

Required: literature matrix or source set and its search scope. Optional: research goal, target users, constraints, and candidate directions. If sources are sparse, state that gap claims are provisional.

## Workflow

1. Check that the literature set records selection scope, verified sources, and relevant comparison dimensions. List material coverage limits.
2. Look for theoretical, methodological, empirical, evaluation, dataset, robustness, and reproducibility gaps. Ground each observation in specific source entries or omissions within the documented scope.
3. Distinguish “no matching work found in this search” from “no one has done this”; do not claim exhaustive absence without exhaustive evidence.
4. For each candidate, explain the unresolved problem, who or what it affects, why it matters, and which existing assumptions or limitations motivate it.
5. Assess likely novelty provisionally: describe what is new relative to closest work and what further search could overturn the assessment.
6. Identify a likely reviewer objection and the experiment, theory, or broader search needed to substantiate the gap.
7. Rank candidates by significance and tractability only when criteria are provided; otherwise present a transparent comparison without false precision.

## Output

For each candidate gap return: category; evidence and source locations; search-scope caveat; why it matters; provisional novelty level and uncertainty; likely reviewer objection; and evidence/experiment/theory needed. Include rejected candidates and the reason when useful.

## Validation

Trace every gap to matrix entries or a stated coverage omission. Check that importance is argued separately from absence, novelty is provisional, and a substantiation route addresses the likely objection. Use [evidence policy](../../shared/evidence-policy.md).

## Failure Modes

- Treating lack of retrieved work as proof of novelty.
- Calling a narrow benchmark extension meaningful without explaining impact.
- Inferring that a limitation in one paper is unresolved across the field.
- Omitting what evidence could refute the proposed gap.

## Research Integrity Rules

Never invent prior work, source limitations, or a search result. Keep observations distinct from interpretation. Scope absence claims to the documented search; mark novelty unresolved when coverage is inadequate. Do not present speculation as an established field-wide need.

## Interaction With Other Skills

Upstream: literature-review and literature matrix. Downstream: research-question-designer turns defensible candidates into testable questions; citation-verifier checks disputed sources; paper-reviewer can assess likely objections.
