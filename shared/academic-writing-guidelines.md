# Academic Writing Guidelines

- Prefer simple, precise, direct academic English and define technical terms where needed.
- State the problem, method, evidence, and limitation explicitly. Keep each claim scoped to the conditions actually studied.
- Preserve technical meaning, notation, equations, citations, and LaTeX commands when editing.
- Separate established facts, this work's observations, interpretation, and speculation.
- Use cautious language when evidence is limited. Avoid “groundbreaking,” “revolutionary,” “clearly superior,” and similar language without direct substantiation.
- Avoid filler, inflated claims, unexplained acronyms, and repeated descriptions.
- Do not invent numerical results, references, theorem statements, implementation details, or limitations. Mark missing information for the author.
- Report negative or mixed findings when they materially affect the conclusions.

For support levels and evidence types, use [evidence-policy.md](evidence-policy.md); for citation handling, use [citation-policy.md](citation-policy.md).
