# Evidence Policy

## Evidence categories

| Category | Examples | Supports |
|---|---|---|
| Direct experimental | Results from the stated protocol, datasets, and baselines | Empirical claims scoped to those conditions |
| Statistical | Effect estimates, uncertainty intervals, tests, multiplicity and sampling details | Claims about uncertainty or statistical evidence when assumptions are justified |
| Theoretical | Definitions, assumptions, derivations, proofs | Theorems or guarantees under stated assumptions |
| Literature | Verified primary sources and systematic comparisons | Prior-work, context, and carefully bounded novelty claims |
| Qualitative | Documented observations, case studies, expert coding | Descriptive or interpretive claims within the sampled context |
| Unsupported interpretation | Plausible explanation without direct evidence | Hypotheses for follow-up, not established conclusions |

## Claim matching

- State the claim and its scope before deciding what evidence is needed.
- Match each claim to the evidence type and source location that can support it.
- “Statistically significant” requires an appropriate performed test and its reporting; numerical improvement alone is insufficient.
- “State of the art” requires a sufficiently broad, current, and fair comparison; otherwise name the compared methods and scope.
- “Robust” and “generalizable” require variation across relevant conditions or domains; one dataset supports only a scoped statement.
- Causal wording requires a design that identifies a causal effect; observational association alone does not.
- Novelty claims require verified related-work coverage and should state search scope and remaining uncertainty.
- When evidence is absent, partial, or unverifiable, downgrade the claim or label it unresolved.
